01 — The headline number

Nvidia turned AI compute into an asset class

$500B

Third-party capital Nvidia and six financial groups intend to mobilise for AI infrastructure, announced 10–11 August.

Nvidia signed a memorandum of understanding with Apollo, BlackRock, Blackstone, Brookfield, Goldman Sachs and KKR to stand up independent computing-financing platforms — dedicated pools of capital that lend across Nvidia's ecosystem of AI labs, enterprises and cloud providers.

The mechanism is the story: financing is collateralised by compute itself, structured through private offerings and bonds issued by special-purpose entities able to raise tens of billions at a time. Part of the intent is to let smaller AI startups borrow to buy GPU capacity rather than fund it from equity. Jensen Huang framed AI compute as an "investable asset class" of AI factories.

03 — Chronology

How the week ran

Two threads dominate: capital formation around compute, and the first real enforcement scaffolding for frontier models on both sides of the Atlantic.

  1. Tue 4 August · Washington

    White House convenes the frontier labs on safety testing

    Executives from OpenAI, Anthropic, Google and Meta met White House officials to review a finalised framework for voluntary safety testing of advanced models. It grows out of a June executive order on AI cybersecurity and offers government early access to certain frontier models for up to 30 days — explicitly opt-in, and explicitly barred from becoming a licensing or preclearance regime. Officials had not published the test metrics or said whether results will be made public.

  2. Thu 6 August · Menlo Park

    Meta ships Muse Spark 1.2; Hadrian raises $1.37B

    Meta released Muse Spark 1.2, and separately fielded a 30B-parameter agent designed to run on a single GPU. Defence-manufacturing firm Hadrian closed a $1.37B Series D at a $7.87B valuation.

  3. Fri 7 August · Hangzhou

    Unitree doubles down on embodied AI ahead of its IPO

    The humanoid-robotics company said it would keep concentrating R&D on large embodied-AI models, reinforcement learning, self-developed core components and high-performance actuators, as it prepares a Shanghai listing seeking roughly ¥6.1B ($904M).

  4. Sat 8 August · Beijing

    ByteDance releases Seedance 2.5

    The latest iteration of ByteDance's video-generation family, one of five models logged by release trackers in the first ten days of August.

  5. Mon 10 August · San Francisco & New York

    Theseus Infrastructure launches; OpenAI ships an offence-grade security model

    Anthropic, Macquarie Asset Management and GIC announced Theseus Infrastructure, a platform that will develop, own and lease purpose-built data centres back to Anthropic under long-term agreements, with Macquarie and GIC funding the majority of the equity per project and an initial focus on the United States. Anthropic committed to paying 100% of grid-upgrade costs and to covering consumer electricity price increases attributable to its demand. The same day, OpenAI released GPT-5.6-Cyber, and reporting emerged of Nvidia's financing alliance.

  6. Tue 11 August · Global

    Nvidia's $500B alliance, Anthropic's $30B run-rate, and the EU's Android order

    Nvidia's six-partner financing package was confirmed. Anthropic disclosed $30B run-rate revenue and expanded its compute partnership with Google and Broadcom for multiple gigawatts of next-generation capacity — most of it sited in the US, extending the $50B American infrastructure commitment it made in November 2025. In Brussels, the DMA produced an order requiring Google to open Android to rival assistants such as Claude and ChatGPT by August 2027.

06 — Rules

The EU AI Act starts biting — selectively

Enforcement by the Commission's AI Office and national authorities began on 2 August 2026. What actually applies now is narrower than the headline suggests: the transparency layer is live, while most high-risk obligations slipped by seventeen months or more.

  • ✓

    Transparency obligations — in force

    Chatbots and interactive AI systems must tell users they are not human. Deepfakes — generated or altered image, video and audio — must be labelled. Non-compliance carries fines up to €15M or 3% of worldwide annual turnover, whichever is higher.

    2 Aug 2026 · Live
  • !

    Annex III stand-alone high-risk systems — deferred

    Recruitment tools, credit scoring, education, law enforcement, border control and critical infrastructure now face full compliance on 2 December 2027 — a seventeen-month extension on the original date.

    2 Dec 2027
  • →

    Annex I embedded AI — deferred further

    AI inside products already covered by EU product-safety law — medical devices, machinery, toys — moves out to 2 August 2028.

    2 Aug 2028
Two regimes, two philosophies. Brussels is enforcing disclosure duties with turnover-scaled fines while postponing its hardest obligations. Washington spent the same week finalising the opposite instrument: a voluntary, opt-in testing framework offering up to 30 days of early government access to frontier models, with a statutory bar on turning it into licensing or preclearance. The EU's separate DMA order — requiring Google to open Android to rival assistants including Claude and ChatGPT by August 2027 — shows the third lever, competition law, arriving before either safety regime fully lands.
07 — The pattern

Labs stopped owning their buildings

08 — Read with care

What these numbers are not

Nvidia's $500B is a memorandum of understanding subject to definitive agreements, not committed capital. Anthropic's $30B is a run-rate — annualised from a recent period, not trailing revenue. Model benchmark scores here are vendor-reported, frequently run on the vendor's own harness, and DeepSeek's agentic-coding comparisons in particular are not apples-to-apples with rivals tested elsewhere. The 95% cyber completion rate measures willingness to answer on an internal eval, not demonstrated capability. Round sizes and valuations are as announced by the companies and their investors.
